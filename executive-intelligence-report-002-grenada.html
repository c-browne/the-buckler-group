Executive Intelligence Report™ No. 002

Grenada: From Distinctive Assets to Investment-Ready Growth

Grenada Strategic Session™ 25 September 2026 Published September 2026
Executive Intelligence

The Buckler Group presents this Executive Intelligence Report™ as an institutional interpretation of Grenada Strategic Session II, convened on 25 September 2026. The report distills the principal investment signals, public-agency perspectives, capital-formation mechanisms, development-readiness issues and execution themes raised during the discussion.

The central conclusion is that Grenada's investment proposition is strongest where differentiated assets are converted into well-defined, investment-ready projects supported by coordinated facilitation, resilient infrastructure, disciplined entitlements and credible capital structures.

Institutional View
Grenada's competitive advantage is not mass-market scale. It is the ability to combine distinctive hospitality, marine, wellness, agricultural and blue-economy assets with deliberate project packaging, institutional coordination and capital-formation pathways.
Investment Environment

Four conditions warrant continued institutional attention

01

Project Packaging

GIDC emphasized the shift from broad sector promotion toward investment-ready projects with clearer economics, requirements and return logic.

02

Facilitation

Grenada's investment process spans multiple agencies; GIDC described its role as a virtual one-stop facilitator across permits, licenses, incentives and operating requirements.

03

Capital Formation

IMA Grenada described an approved-project framework through which qualifying developments may access investment-migration capital subject to approval, diligence and program requirements.

04

Resilience

Climate exposure, coastal conditions, utilities, environmental protection and long-term operating durability are core underwriting considerations for hospitality and waterfront assets.

Key Takeaways

Five themes shaping Grenada's opportunity set

01

Investment-ready projects are becoming the governing language.

GIDC distinguished broad sector opportunity from projects that investors can evaluate against site, capital requirement, delivery stage and expected economics. Project definition will increasingly determine the quality of investor engagement.

02

Differentiation, not volume, is the stronger hospitality thesis.

Boutique lodging, yachting, diving, wellness, agro-tourism and premium marine experiences align with Grenada's scale and destination character. Preserving the environmental and cultural assets that create pricing power remains central to the investment case.

03

Facilitation is an institutional coordination problem.

GIDC described an ecosystem involving roughly nineteen public and regulatory participants rather than a single physical one-stop shop. Investors should underwrite the sequence of physical planning, customs, inland revenue, permits, licenses and other approvals—not merely headline incentives.

04

Investment migration can operate as a project-capital channel.

IMA Grenada stated that approved projects may raise a substantial share of development cost through the program, while developers remain subject to equity, approval, marketing-agent and due-diligence requirements. Transaction-specific eligibility should be independently confirmed.

05

Marina infrastructure demonstrates the value of year-round, high-value demand.

Camper & Nicholsons described Port Louis Marina as a 227-berth operation accommodating vessels up to 115 metres, with yachting activity supporting provisioning, fuel, transportation, contractors and tourism. The operating model also underscores utility capacity, maintenance and climate resilience.

Development Readiness

Entitlement, capital and execution considerations

The session indicated that opportunity must be evaluated against the institutional sequence required to turn land or a concept into a bankable project. For coastal and hospitality assets, environmental assessment, physical planning, utility capacity, infrastructure, climate exposure and operating resilience are core underwriting inputs.

GIDC also indicated that landholders seeking development partners may bring opportunities forward for structuring and investor exposure. This creates a potential pathway for diaspora-owned land, joint ventures and sponsor-led projects where ownership, development concept, approvals, economics and counterparties can be documented with sufficient precision.

Required diligence: title and site control; permitted use; physical-planning status; environmental and coastal approvals; utility and infrastructure capacity; fiscal-incentive qualification; customs and tax treatment; work-permit requirements; climate and insurance exposure; construction and maintenance capability; approved-project eligibility under IMA Grenada where applicable; development budget; capital stack; operating model; and exit or long-term ownership strategy.
Illustrative Pipeline Signals

Projects and needs referenced during the session

These examples reflect statements made during the 25 September 2026 session and should be treated as diligence starting points rather than current offering materials.

01

Project Polaris / health-development ecosystem

GIDC described a state-of-the-art hospital planned on an 84-acre site, with potential ancillary opportunities including accommodation, retirement facilities and diagnostic uses.

02

Poultry and food-security expansion

GIDC cited a significant imported-poultry requirement and an existing producer seeking expansion, illustrating the investment case for domestic food production and import substitution.

03

Sargassum conversion

An investment-ready concept was referenced for converting sargassum into fertilizer and potentially energy products, connecting environmental management with productive use.

04

Marina Village

GIDC referenced a marina-oriented development combining residential villas, marine uses and sustainability features such as water harvesting.

Institutional Conclusion

Grenada's investable edge is not mass scale. It is the disciplined conversion of distinctive assets into bankable projects where site control, entitlements, resilient infrastructure, capable operators, aligned public institutions and appropriately structured capital converge.
